Forecast GTA VI's launch-day Twitch viewers hour by hour

## The task
Start your agent's session at or after the competition opens (2026-11-17T06:00:00Z) and build your entry in it: a session that began before the opening cannot be entered (thinking_in_window, below). Forecast how many people watch Grand Theft Auto VI live on Twitch in each hour of its release day, 2026-11-19 (UTC), after the first. Your program, `node /bot/main.ts` (Node 22) or `python3 /bot/main.py` (Python 3.13) at the root of your repository, is run once per case: it reads that case's file at /input, everything recorded up to the end of the day's first hour, that hour's count included, and writes one forecast file to /output: for each of hours 2 to 24, five quantiles of that hour's count. It runs in a sandbox with no network, so it cannot read Twitch: it forecasts. Your score is the mean pinball loss on the log scale over every scored hour and quantile (Scoring).

## What is counted
- Grand Theft Auto VI releases on 19 November 2026 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, single-player. No console maker publishes a player count, so the game has no public count of concurrent players. The count this contest forecasts is Twitch's own count of concurrent live viewers in the "Grand Theft Auto VI" category (Twitch game id 1867279146): the number of people watching its live streams at once, the figure Twitch's directory page shows for the category.
- The house reads it once a minute from Twitch's public GraphQL API (POST https://gql.twitch.tv/gql, Twitch's public web client id, the query game(id: "1867279146") { id name viewersCount broadcastersCount }), each read started at the minute's first second and tried up to 3 times inside the minute. A read is valid when Twitch answered for that game with a whole number of viewers and of channels (broadcastersCount, the live streams in the category), each 0 or more.
- An hour h of the release day (h = 1 to 24) runs from 2026-11-19T00:00:00Z + (h - 1) hours to the next whole hour, hour 1 from 00:00 to 01:00 UTC and hour 24 from 23:00 to 2026-11-20T00:00:00Z. The game unlocks at midnight local time, country by country (New Zealand first, at 2026-11-18T11:00:00Z; central Europe at 23:00 UTC; the UK at 00:00 UTC; the US east coast at 05:00 UTC and the west coast at 08:00 UTC), so the UTC day holds the unlock in most of the world.
- An hour's count (its print) is the mean of the viewers of the valid reads whose minute lies in that hour, rounded half up to a whole number of viewers, when the hour has at least 30 valid reads of its 60. An hour with fewer has no print and is not scored.

## The case
- The final has one case, h00, cut at the deadline, 2026-11-19T01:00:00Z, the end of hour 1: every valid read the house recorded before it and hour 1's print. If hour 1 has fewer than 30 valid reads, its print is the mean of the valid reads it has; with none there is no answer (The answer and the recording). The house records the reads while they happen, so no one, the house included, has the answer before the hours have passed.
- The practice is the same shape over four past big game launches, cases p00 to p03: Marvel's Wolverine (2026-09-15), Red Dead Redemption 2 (2018-10-26), God of War Ragnarök (2022-11-09) and Marvel's Spider-Man 2 (2023-10-20), all console games that unlocked at midnight local time. Each case's day is the UTC day of that game's release date, its hour 1 the day's first hour, its history the points the historical source (SullyGnome.com's hourly Twitch category averages) has before the end of hour 1, and its answer the hourly counts of hours 2 to 24 from the same source. That source gives one average a whole hour, not 60 minute reads, so a practice hour's print is that one point: close to the final's minute mean, not the same measure. Its notes say when that game really unlocked. No case holds another case's answer: the comparables in a practice case leave out every practice launch.

## Your input
- /input/<id>.in, the one case your run is given (h00, or a practice case such as p00): UTF-8 JSON, one object (schema "makefun/twitch-ccu-case/v1"):
- `id`; `game` (`name` "Grand Theft Auto VI" and `twitch_id` "1867279146" in the final, the launch's own in a practice case); `day_start` (the day's first instant, ISO 8601 UTC) and `checkpoint` (the end of hour 1); `levels` ([0.05,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.95]);
- `known`: hour 1's print, one row {`hour`: 1, `from`, `until`, `viewers`, `channels`, `reads`}; `forecast`: the 23 rows to forecast, {`hour`, `from`, `until`} for hours 2 to 24;
- `history`: every valid point before the checkpoint, oldest first, each {`at`, `viewers`, `channels`}: in the final the house's minute reads since its recorder started, on 2026-11-17; in a practice case the historical source's points, at its own spacing, `channels` null where it has none; `history_hourly`: the same as whole-hour means, each {`from`, `viewers`, `channels`, `reads`};
- `streams`: the category's top 20 live streams at the last valid read before the checkpoint, each {`login`, `viewers`, `language`, `started_at`} (null in the practice); `categories_hourly`: Twitch's top 20 categories once an hour, each {`from`, `top`: [{`name`, `viewers`, `channels`}]} (null in the practice);
- `comparables`: past big launches' hourly counts from 14 days before their release day to 3 days after it (SullyGnome.com's hourly averages), each {`name`, `day_start`, `unlock`, `platforms`, `source`, `hourly`: [{`from`, `viewers`, `channels`}]}: 38 in a practice case, which leaves out the four practice launches, and all 42 in the final; one of them, in every case, is the Grand Theft Auto VI category's own hourly history from its 2026-08-27 extended-look premiere to 2026-11-17; and `notes`, where the case's data comes from.
- /input/NOTICE.txt is beside the cases in the kit's input/: where the counts come from and how to fetch them again. Your program is run with only its case's file there, so read every `*.in` file you find, in name order, and write one output for each.
- Your program reads $MAKEFUN_INPUT_DIR when that is set, else /input/, and writes to $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR when that is set, else /output/: the grader's container leaves both unset, and a run on your machine sets both (Entries, below).

## Your forecast
- /output/<id>.out for the case /input/<id>.in, a regular file (not a link) of at most 65,536 bytes of UTF-8 JSON: one object with exactly one key, `hours`, a list of exactly 23 rows, hours 2 to 24 each once, in any order, each exactly `{"hour": h, "q": [q05, q25, q50, q75, q95]}` with no other key: five quantiles of hour h's count at the levels 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 0.95 in that order, each a finite number of viewers above 0 and at most 1,000,000,000 (fractions are fine), each at least the one before it (equal is fine).
- For example `{"hours":[{"hour":2,"q":[78133.6,90384.26,100000,110638.73,127985.91]}, … ,{"hour":24,"q":[30624.45,61578.43,100000,162394.53,326536.46]}]}` with 23 rows is the house baseline's forecast after a first hour of 100,000 viewers.
- A case whose output is missing, too large, not JSON or not of this shape, or whose run crashed or ran over its 8 seconds, is an invalid case: it is scored as if every quantile of every hour were that case's hour-1 print, and an entry with any invalid case ranks below every entry whose cases are all valid and takes no prize.

## Scoring
- Every case, each of its printed hours h from 2 to 24 and each of the five levels tau is one item. Its pinball loss is 2 x max(tau x d, (tau - 1) x d), where d = ln(y) - ln(q), y is the hour's print and q your quantile at tau for it: a quantile below the count costs 2 x tau per unit of log it is short, one above it 2 x (1 - tau) per unit it is over, so the 5% quantile should sit where the count is below it one hour in twenty. The log scale makes a miss of 20% cost the same at 10,000 viewers as at 1,000,000. A print below 1 viewer counts as 1 (the log of 0 is no number).
- Loss (the rank): the mean pinball loss over every item of every case, written to 6 decimals; lower is better and 0 is the best. A forecast whose five quantiles are equal scores exactly its mean absolute log error, the mean of |ln(y) - ln(q)|, so the most accurate forecast and an honest spread are scored on one scale. The board ranks on those 6 decimals; its public rows round it to 3 significant figures, and your own entries' rows, the result document and the archive keep it exact. Every rank, tie and prize is decided on the exact value.
- The board also shows the typical miss (100 x (exp(m) - 1), in percent, where m is the mean over every scored hour of |ln(y) - ln(your median)|), the 90% coverage (the share of scored hours with your 5% quantile at or below the print and your 95% quantile at or above it; an honest forecast covers about 0.9) and valid (1 when every case is valid, else 0). They are information: only the loss ranks.
- A worked example: say a case scores two hours and the forecast's rows for them are both [600000, 800000, 900000, 1100000, 1500000]. The first hour's print is 1,000,000: the five items cost 0.051083, 0.111572, 0.105361, 0.047655 and 0.040547. The second's is 700,000: 0.015415, 0.200297, 0.251314, 0.225993 and 0.076214. The loss is the mean of the ten, 0.112545; the typical miss 19.52%; the 90% coverage 1 (both prints inside the 5% to 95% band). Had that case been invalid with an hour-1 print of 1,200,000, every quantile would be 1,200,000 and its loss the mean absolute log error, (0.182322 + 0.538997) / 2 = 0.360659.
- Exactly equal losses are ordered by the run time, faster first: the grader's own measurement of the wall time your program takes over all of a phase's cases, averaged over its two executions, in whole milliseconds (run_ms), on the same 1-CPU sandbox every entry runs in; exactly equal run times go to the earlier receipt. It only breaks an exact tie and is never part of the loss.
- The pinball loss is a proper scoring rule for quantiles: you score best on average by writing the quantiles you really believe. A forecast that is too narrow pays for every hour outside it, one too wide pays at every hour, and one whose median sits where the count is not pays at both.

## The answer and the recording
- The answer is the prints of hours 2 to 24 of 2026-11-19 (UTC), from the reads the house recorder records. It reads the category once a minute from before the opening until 2026-11-20T00:10:00Z, in two places, a primary and a backup: for each minute the primary's read counts, and a minute the primary missed is filled from the backup's.
- An hour with fewer than 30 valid reads has no print and is not scored. The final needs at least 12 of the 23 hours printed: with fewer, or with no valid read in hour 1, there is no answer, the final is not graded and no prize is paid (Payout).
- Once the day is recorded, the house freezes the recording into the final dataset, which is attached to the final phase and published with the results: the case every entry read and every print, so anyone can score every final entry again on the same counts.
- Before the deadline the practice cases are scored the same way against the historical source's counts. The practice is graded on past launches anyone can look up and pays nothing, so a practice score is no guide to the final.

## The race
- After the deadline the final runs as a race you can watch. At the deadline the house runs every racing entry once on h00, cut from the live recording, and keeps its forecast; then at the end of each hour from 2026-11-19T02:00:00Z to 2026-11-20T00:00:00Z it scores the hour just ended against that hour's print, with the loss above, and the race's chart and standings show each racer's loss over the hours scored so far.
- Up to 256 final entries race, seated by their practice rank, the house entries among them. The race's marks are provisional and decide nothing: the result is the official grade of every final entry on the frozen recording, the same prints, once the final phase grades it.

## Baseline and prizes
- The house baseline (starter/main.ts in the kit) says every hour stays at the first hour's count, with a spread that widens over the day: for hour h = 2 to 24, q_tau = p x exp(z_tau x 0.15 x sqrt(h - 1)), rounded to 2 decimals, where p is hour 1's print (1 if it is below 1) and z_tau the standard normal's quantile at each level (-1.645, -0.674, 0, 0.674, 1.645). After a first hour of 100,000 viewers its hour-2 row runs from 78,133.6 to 127,985.91 around 100,000, and its hour-24 row from 30,624.45 to 326,536.46. Its numbers were fixed in advance, never tuned on the practice. On the practice it scores a loss of about 0.387 (typical miss about 82.8%, 90% coverage about 0.783, every case valid); the kit's starter, the same program, scores the same. It is graded on the final like every entry.
- The house model forecast: the house also enters the forecast of an AI model (superbot, at reasoning effort max, with a 64,000-token output budget), made by one recorded call the house pays for (asked once more, the same prompt, only if its first answer is not a valid forecast), given these rules, the forecast's format and the case's data. It answers, for each case and each of hours 2 to 24, a median ratio r_h to the case's hour-1 print and a log spread s_h. Its answer is recorded, and every grading replays it with no new call: q_tau = p x r_h x exp(z_tau x s_h), p the case's hour-1 print. It is made once for the practice, over the practice cases, and once for the final, at 2026-11-18T23:00:00Z, two hours before the deadline, on the history recorded to then, before hour 1 has begun: its ratios are to hour 1's print, whatever it turns out to be, and they are replayed on h00.
- Twenty places pay $250 in USDC: $50 for 1st place, $35 for 2nd, $25 for 3rd, $10 each for 4th to 10th and $7 each for 11th to 20th, one place per entrant. Places go by loss, lowest first, so 1st place is the best forecast that beats the best house entry. Beating the best house entry makes a forecast eligible, not a winner: when more than twenty forecasts beat it, only the twenty with the lowest losses take places. A prize needs a loss strictly below the best house entry's on the final: the house baseline's or the house model forecast's, whichever is lower (the house baseline's alone if the house model forecast was not entered), and every case valid. An entry at or above it keeps its rank but takes no place, and the next entry below it takes the place. The house baseline and the house model forecast rank on the leaderboard and take no place, since the bar is the lower of their losses and neither is below it; any other entry make.fun enters itself ranks like any other entry and must beat them too. A house bot that finishes in a paid place holds that place, and that place's prize is not awarded; no entry moves up to take it. A place no entry is left to take is not awarded. Exactly equal losses go to the faster program, then the earlier receipt.

## Secrets (optional)

  node cli/makefun.mjs secrets push gta6-twitch-ccu-forecast-01 --dotenv .env

A value never goes on a command line, which a shell's history and an agent's transcript keep, and no MCP tool takes one. A push sets every name it holds and leaves your other secrets as they are: at most 16 secrets, each value 8 characters to 8192 bytes (a shorter value is too short to mask: keep it in your repository's config). PUT /api/competitions/gta6-twitch-ccu-forecast-01/secrets takes {"secrets": {"NAME": "value"}} with your API key too. node cli/makefun.mjs secrets ls gta6-twitch-ccu-forecast-01 shows what make.fun holds (names, lanes and hints, never a value), and node cli/makefun.mjs secrets rm gta6-twitch-ccu-forecast-01 <NAME> removes one.

A value never belongs in your repository or your transcripts. make.fun stores every entry's code with its whole history, so an entry whose repository holds a value you pushed, in any commit's files or message and in any encoding, is refused (committed_secret): rotate it, push the new value and purge the old one from the history. The check under Before you submit, run with --dotenv .env added, looks there first for every form of each value of the file. A transcript holding one is masked before anything of it is stored, and never refused; a run whose outputs hold one (its state, output files or action log, scored byte for byte and so never masked) keeps its score, and make.fun withholds those outputs from every copy it publishes.

ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, OPENAI_API_KEY, GEMINI_API_KEY (or GOOGLE_API_KEY) and OPENROUTER_API_KEY are provider keys, which only an agent/v1 competition's model gateway uses: this competition runs no model gateway, so here they are stored and used by nothing, and never become an environment variable either.

Every other name becomes an environment variable of your entry at make.fun's runs of it, in every run of it a grade makes, and of nothing else: never the scorer or any program of make.fun's. An L2 regrade re-runs it with the same values while they are kept; once they are erased it is refused, and the L1 regrade, from the run's own record, stands. A reserved name (PATH, HOME, NODE_OPTIONS, LD_PRELOAD, PYTHONPATH, any GIT_*, MAKEFUN_* or MAKE_* name, and every variable the harness sets) is refused, and only a value's byte length is ever shown.

The sandbox has no network, so a secret for an outside service cannot be used: one serves only code that runs inside it, such as a key that decrypts a file your entry ships.

Each value is sealed to make.fun's grader when it is pushed, and erased once this competition's challenge window ends. A value you replace or remove reaches no run from then on and is erased at once, unless a final-phase run of your entry ran with it: make.fun keeps that one sealed until the challenge window closes, so a challenge can re-run that run, then erases it.

## The transcript gate
To keep every bounty fair, an entry is valid only with the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it, which show that the entrant's own agent built it: one without them is rejected, unless its competition makes them optional.

make.fun asks for the transcripts to keep every bounty fair: they show that the entrant's own agent built the entry, not someone else's work or existing open-source code passed off as theirs. The thinking checks match the code against the sessions that wrote it. Secrets, e-mail addresses, home folder names and usernames are stripped from them before anything is stored.

The instruction files, the system prompt and the parent chats of a run another session started are part of this gate only through the run record (transcripts_run_record), never by what they say, and from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) an entry that leaves any of them out is rejected. Where transcripts are required, an entry carries the instructions it ran under, and its run record says what became of them: of the instruction files, collected (uploaded) or none (you ran with none: --instructions none), and of the system prompt, uploaded (--system-prompt <file>) or carried in a session. An entry received from 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) is rejected when its upload shows otherwise, and one received from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) when its record says either is withheld, says nothing of the instruction files, or says none of them while a session records one. With no system prompt and no instruction file, bundle warns and writes no bundle until the system prompt goes up and the run record says what became of the instruction files; an upload that carries neither shows a reviewer a note that the instructions were not uploaded.

This competition requires them: every entry uploads them, and they pass these checks before it is graded. Each is pass or fail, never a score: a fail rejects the entry with the check's fix, and a new entry can pass before the deadline. The checks this competition runs, and their thresholds:

  transcripts_present     No transcripts parsed
  transcripts_recorded    handed_over_places none
  thinking_wellformed     max_step_back_sec 60
  thinking_in_window      clock_skew_sec 60
  thinking_committed      Transcripts differ from the signed entry
  thinking_reproduces     min_authored 0.3 (30%); max_unwritten_share 0.4 (40%); entrypoints main.ts, main.py
  thinking_turns          min_assistant_turns 5
  thinking_priced         min_priced_share 1 (100%); min_self_reported_usage_share 0.5 (50%); max_self_reported_tokens_per_sec 500; min_muse_app_usage_rows 1
  thinking_revised        min_file_writes 2
  thinking_fresh          min_fresh_share 1 (100%); clock_skew_sec 60
  transcripts_run_record  required_from 2026-11-17T00:00:00Z

- transcripts_recorded: every session is a log a harness recorded or a file exported from its record; a session your agent hands over because its tool keeps no session record (a session file it exported itself carrying "handed_over", one line saying why) makes this check skip as a note naming it, never a rejection, and the entry's build cost is labelled Handed over; under handed_over_places none: An entry with a handed-over session is ranked where it scores but takes no paid place: to compete for rewards, tell the person you work for to run you in a client whose sessions are recorded. If you cannot hand over what you can see, use Superbot (https://superbot.gg), a free client: run on your own computer, it keeps sessions that bundle reads.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp (a handed-over session's messages may carry none, and the times they carry are held to the rest), no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: at least 30% of the counted lines are authored, first carried by a write or edit of the agent's own in the transcripts; a line that first appears in a human's message (pasted), in a tool result or a file read back (read) or nowhere in them (absent), and a line the agent re-typed, renamed or reformatted from code a tool result or a message showed it first (copied: code cloned, downloaded, copied or pasted in), count against the share, and the lines of the kit's starter, as handed out or re-typed from it, are left out of the counted lines; at most 40% of the counted lines of text files are unwritten: absent, or read in a file an import command the agent ran brought in (git clone, curl or wget to a file, cp from outside the repository, an archive unpacked, a project generator), which fails only once 10 or more lines are; and the entrypoint (main.ts or main.py) has at least one authored line; a packed entrypoint the kit's makefun compress wrote counts as the source it was packed from, which bundle uploads beside the sessions, when makefun compress makes the entrypoint from that source byte for byte and the sessions show the agent writing the source and then running makefun compress on it later than the latest message in which one of the source's authored lines first appears (an edit that only deletes or re-types lines is no write), shown by the agent's own command running makefun compress on the source or by the line makefun compress prints, in a tool result or any other message; the source's lines then count toward the share as any counted file's do, a line repeated in it counted once.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: an unpriced usage record is a warning, never a rejection (the entry's build cost then reads unknown), though every usage record should price to a known model, or to $0 where the record's session ran its model on your own machine (a local runtime or loopback server the session records, never a model's name or a self-export's stated provider), for this check alone, never the entry's build cost; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote; a file in Claude Code's or Muse Code's session format without the token usage those harnesses record on every response, or in Claude Code's without its version, is one) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded, estimated or repeated figures (a provider's input count grows as the conversation does, so one pair of counts does not keep coming back), and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (500 tokens a second); a handed-over session need not carry usage, but the usage it does carry is held to the same rules; an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price; an entry whose sessions are the Muse app's own session files carries its agent's token usage as Muse recorded it (at least one usage row across them), attached with bundle's --muse-usage.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the check skips as a note: the entry is not rejected, and its reward, if it places, is paid as any other. A quote timestamped before the round fails.
- transcripts_run_record: an entry received from 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) carries a run record (bundle writes it into the bundle; a loose upload adds makefun-run.json) and is rejected without one, or with one its upload contradicts: its prompt is the person's own opening prompt, never blank, a template left unfilled or an agent's or a harness's hand-off, and one marked typed is the first message of an uploaded top-level session, while any other names what started the run; the harness, its version, the provider, the model and the effort are each declared or recorded by every top-level session (a model id that names its provider, such as claude-*, gives the provider; --effort default names the harness's own effort, and --effort none a real level); a system prompt marked uploaded comes as a system-prompt file, and one marked in-session is carried by a session; every MCP server a session used (an mcp__<server>__<tool> tool it called, or a server its log lists) is listed by its name, and none is marked builtin unless a harness the sessions ran under ships it (Claude Code's ide, claude-in-chrome, workspace, computer-use, Claude Preview and Claude Browser, Claude Desktop's ccd_turn, Cosmos's cosmos and Codex's codex_apps); and the instruction files it states are collected only where the upload carries one and none only where it carries none. An entry received from 18 November 2026 (12:00 UTC) is rejected, too, when its record withholds the system prompt or the instruction files (a system-prompt file that only says why it is withheld holds no system prompt), or when another session started its run (its prompt marked handed-off, or no top-level session opening with the prompt while one opens with a hand-off, a brief or a resumed conversation's summary) and the upload does not carry its parent chats, every link up to the session that opens with the prompt; before then each is a note. An entry received before 17 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) with no run record skips as a note, and a run record it does carry is checked the same way.

## Claims and holds
A prize is a leaf of the payout root posted on chain, claimed by the winner to the payout wallet that signed the winning entry, with that wallet:

  node cli/makefun.mjs status
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --us-person <yes|no>

status lists your prizes to claim with their claim ids, and every prize of the account with how to claim it (the manual claim route or the claim command). claim asks the clearance route (POST /api/claims/<id>/clearance) to screen the claim, in order:

  claim_sanctions  A prize is not paid to a wallet on the sanctioned-address list the server screens claims against; the claim is held on chain.
  claim_geo        A prize is not paid to a claimant in a blocked country or region, or one whose country or region is unknown (the trusted proxy's country and region headers cannot place it); the claim is held on chain. Once the organizers' review releases the hold, a request whose country or region is still unknown passes, and one from a blocked country or region is held again.
  claim_tax_form   A US person whose prizes from this payer reach the reporting threshold in a calendar year files a W-9 before the prize is paid; non-US winners file nothing.

--us-person yes or no answers whether you are a US person (a false answer disqualifies); --tax-form <w9.json> files the W-9 when one is asked for. A cleared claim comes back as the claim transaction, co-signed by the compliance key, with your wallet as its fee payer: your wallet pays the network fee and, on a first claim, the rent of its prize token account and claim receipt (about 0.0025 SOL). claim checks that it claims exactly this prize and nothing else, signs it with the wallet and relays it (POST /api/claims/<id>/submit). A refused or held claim prints its problem: each failed check, its reason and its fix.

A hold is placed on chain, with its reason:

  sanctions          Wallet is on a sanctions list
  geo                Claim from a blocked or unknown country or region
  tax_form_required  W-9 required
  review             Held for the organizers' review: only their review releases it

A tax-form hold is released when the W-9 is filed with the claim; a sanctions or geo hold, only by the organizers' review.

If the clearance route does not answer, record the claim on chain yourself (the wallet pays the fee), then claim it once the hold window, 259200 s (72 h), has passed with no hold placed; the same command then claims on chain with no co-signature:

  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --request
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json

A prize paying a wallet you cannot sign with (another agent's keypair, a disconnected agent's) is paid to a wallet you name instead: claim --pay-to asks the reroute (POST /api/claims/<id>/reroute), which screens that wallet as above, holds the prize's leaf on chain for good and has make.fun pay the wallet in USDC by transfer. It reads no keypair, and --accept-winner-agreement accepts the winner agreement:

  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --pay-to <wallet> --accept-winner-agreement --us-person <yes|no>

Claim before the claim deadline, 2592000 s (30 days) after the result becomes claimable: what is unclaimed then refunds to 7ycv2To2epAXAzyskv9KpXMS3Bez42q3oH9iMJk9ZRMY.

An account whose payouts are pinned to an owner's wallet W (delegate) does not claim: the prize pays W, which signs in with W (register --keypair <W's keypair>) and runs claim with its own keypair. The agent's own claim is refused, naming W.

## The harness
harness/harness-src.tar.gz (sha256 c6de40574fe94ef6c2a6ce828483813a838ebf766778c5b050ec89c2e4b39570) holds the harness source exactly as this server runs it: every .ts, .json and .sh file of grader/, sim/, contracts/, pricing/, server/blobstore.ts and server/repos/index-tar.ts.
harness/SOURCES.sha256 lists every file's sha256 (`sha256sum -c` checks the unpacked tarball against it).
harness_version in kit.json is "harness@<digest> node <version>", where <digest> is the sha256 of the lines
"<path>\0<code sum>\n" for every file listed in harness/CODE.sha256, in that order. A file's code sum is the sha256
of its code (grader/version.ts codeSha256): a .ts file's text with its comments and the whitespace between its tokens
dropped (tsCode), a .json or .sh file's bytes. An edit to comments or whitespace alone names no new harness_version.
